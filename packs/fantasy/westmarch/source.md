# The Westmarch

The Westmarch is a frontier region at the edge of the kingdom, where the
king's road ends and the old growth begins. It stretches roughly forty miles
east to west, from the River Seil to the Greywood's impenetrable canopy, and
perhaps thirty miles north to south, hemmed by the Ashfall Ridge to the north
and open scrubland fading into unclaimed steppe to the south.

## Settlements and Landmarks

**Thornwall** is the largest settlement, a timber-palisade town of perhaps
three hundred souls built where the king's road meets the River Seil. A
stone bridge — the only crossing for twenty miles — makes Thornwall the
chokepoint for all trade moving west. The town has a mill, a smithy, a
chapel to the Quiet God, and a garrison tower manned by a dozen soldiers
of the Crown Watch. The market square fills on seventh-days with trappers,
herbalists, and the occasional merchant caravan brave enough to come this
far.

**Seil's Crossing** is the bridge itself, old enough that nobody remembers
who built it. The stonework predates the kingdom by centuries. Locals leave
offerings — river-polished stones, braided grass — on the bridge posts at
dusk. Some say the bridge won't let certain people cross after dark.

**Ashenford** sits half a day's ride north along the ridge trail, a cluster
of homesteads around a communal well and a timber longhall. The people here
are miners and charcoal-burners who work the lower slopes of the Ashfall
Ridge. They are insular, suspicious of outsiders, and fiercely loyal to
Warden Maren, who keeps the peace with an iron hand and a fair tongue.

**The Greywood Edge** marks the western boundary of settled land. Beyond
the last woodcutter's shack, the forest thickens into something older and
less welcoming. Hunters who range too deep report strange silences, trees
that seem to have moved between visits, and the occasional ruin half-
swallowed by root and moss. A collapsed watchtower on the tree line — the
Broken Spire — serves as a landmark and an informal border marker.

## Factions

The **Crown Watch** maintains order in the Westmarch under Captain Aldric
Hale, a career soldier posted here as a quiet punishment for a political
disagreement he refuses to discuss. The Watch numbers only sixteen soldiers,
spread thin between Thornwall's garrison tower and irregular patrols. Hale
wants to extend patrols deeper into the Greywood, convinced that the recent
troubles originate there, but he lacks the numbers. The Crown Watch answers
to the distant capital and receives supply caravans every two months — when
the roads are passable.

The **Seil Compact** is an informal alliance of Westmarch settlers, hunters,
and traders who believe the Crown Watch is ineffective and that the frontier
folk must look after their own. Their unofficial leader is Renna Voss, a
former trapper turned provisioner in Thornwall. The Compact pools resources,
shares information, and occasionally takes matters into its own hands — border
justice, they call it. Hale considers them vigilantes. Voss considers the
Watch a garrison of strangers who don't understand the land.

The tension between Watch and Compact simmers constantly. Both want the
Westmarch safe; they disagree bitterly about who should protect it and how.

## People

**Captain Aldric Hale** commands the Crown Watch garrison. He is methodical,
stubborn, and privately worried. The posting was meant to be a backwater
sinecure, but something is wrong in the Greywood and he doesn't have the
resources to find out what. He trusts procedure over instinct and parchment
over hearsay. He will reluctantly accept help from outsiders, provided they
report to him.

**Renna Voss** runs the dry-goods store in Thornwall and leads the Seil
Compact. She is sharp, pragmatic, and deeply rooted in the Westmarch. Her
grandmother was one of the original settlers. Voss doesn't trust the Crown
or anyone who speaks for it, but she will work with anyone who puts the
frontier first.

**Warden Maren** governs Ashenford with quiet authority. She is old enough
to remember when the Greywood was just a forest. She knows more about the
land than she lets on and speaks in careful, measured sentences. She trades
information sparingly, like currency.

**Corin Blackthorn** is a young ranger who operates alone in the Greywood
fringe. He reports to no one. He has seen things in the deep forest that he
won't fully describe — moving lights, structures that shouldn't exist, tracks
that end abruptly. He is skittish, competent, and exhausted.

**Dalla the Smith** works the Thornwall forge. She is massive, cheerful, and
one of the few people in town trusted by both the Watch and the Compact. She
repairs weapons, shoes horses, and hears everything. She is also quietly
terrified: her apprentice went into the Greywood three weeks ago and hasn't
come back.

**Old Gerren** is the chapel keeper in Thornwall, a retired soldier who
found religion late in life. He tends the shrine to the Quiet God and
dispenses advice that is sometimes useful and always long-winded. He has a
surprisingly detailed knowledge of the region's history and the old ruins.

## Threads

The **Greywood Silence** is the central tension. Something has changed in
the old forest. Hunters report that game has fled the deep woods. Sound
behaves strangely past the Broken Spire. Two people have gone missing in
the last month — Dalla's apprentice and a Compact scout named Pel. No
bodies have been found. Hale wants to organize an expedition; Voss wants
to send her own people first, before the Watch tramples the evidence.

The **Bridge Dispute** is a simmering conflict over toll rights at Seil's
Crossing. The Crown Watch has begun collecting a crossing tax to fund
additional patrols. The Compact argues the bridge predates the kingdom and
no one has the right to tax it. Merchants are caught in the middle, and
at least one caravan has turned back rather than pay. The dispute is a
proxy for the larger question: who owns the Westmarch?

The **Ridge Mines** thread involves Ashenford and the Ashfall Ridge. The
miners have broken into an older tunnel system beneath the ridge — clearly
worked by someone, long ago. Warden Maren has quietly sealed the entrance
and forbidden further digging, but some miners are restless. Whatever
was mined down there, it wasn't iron or copper. Strange tools have been
found. Maren knows more than she's saying.

## Mood

The Westmarch is a place where the familiar meets the uncanny. The
settlements are rough but genuine — smoke and sweat and honest labor.
The land beyond is old and patient and not entirely knowable. The people
are practical, tired, and wary. They've built something real on the edge
of the world, and now something is pressing back. The mood is windswept,
iron-grey, and quietly tense — a breath held too long.
