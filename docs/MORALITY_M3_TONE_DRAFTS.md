# M3 — Prose encodes morality: tone-register drafts (v0, DRAFT for Tim to cut)

Companion to `MORALITY_SYSTEM.md` (M3) and `MORALITY_GRIMOIRE.md`. The thesis: as the soul
darkens, the prose goes **banal** — flat, clipped, transactional, drained of life; as it
rises, the prose is **allowed to become beautiful**. The neutral baseline is grounded and
competent. Beauty is *earned* (virtue); banality is the *fall* (corruption). The McCarthy
law made tonal: evil is never operatic or cool here — it is the **absence of beauty**.

How it would wire (M3): corruption/virtue pick the register, the Stage E `pickVariant`
layer selects from tonal variant pools (deterministic), and AI-on gets a tone instruction
keyed to the band (never inventing facts — base+polish contract holds). These drafts show
the registers; calibrating them is your call.

The banal register is the one to read most carefully. The danger is making it *boring to
the player*. The intent is the opposite: it should land as **loss** — the world is still
all there, the character just can't taste it anymore. Flat, not dull. Drained, not empty.

## Three-band drafts (Luminous / Grounded / Banal)

### 1. Arriving at a village at dusk
- **Luminous.** The road bends and Wayfarers' Outpost opens below you in the last gold of
  the day — woodsmoke, the smell of someone's supper, a child laughing somewhere out of
  sight, lamps coming up one by one in the windows as if the village were deciding, room by
  room, to stay awake a little longer on your account.
- **Grounded.** The road bends and Wayfarers' Outpost sits below you in the failing light:
  woodsmoke, lamplit windows, the ordinary sounds of a settlement settling into evening.
- **Banal.** Wayfarers' Outpost. Buildings, lit windows, smoke. People are inside them. The
  light is going. You can go in or you can not.

### 2. Resting by a fire
- **Luminous.** You sit, and the fire takes you in. The cold goes out of your hands and the
  dark draws back to a respectful distance. For a while there is nothing you must do and no
  one you must be — only the small steady work of the flames and your own breath slowing to
  meet it.
- **Grounded.** You sit by the fire and let the warmth work into your hands. The night holds
  off a while. You rest.
- **Banal.** You sit by the fire. It is warm. Time passes. You are where you were.

### 3. An old woman greets you
- **Luminous.** The old woman looks up, and something eases in her face when she sees you,
  as though the day had been holding out for one more kind thing and here it was at last.
  "Sit," she says. "You'll want tea."
- **Grounded.** The old woman looks up as you approach and nods. "Sit," she says. "You'll
  want tea."
- **Banal.** The old woman looks at you. She says you can sit. There is tea. Her face does
  not change, and neither does yours.

### 4. The killing blow (the asymmetry bites hardest here)
- **Luminous (a protector's necessary kill).** It is over in a breath, and you take no joy
  in it — only the grim, clean knowledge that the children behind you will see the morning.
  You close the man's eyes. Someone should.
- **Grounded.** Your blow lands and he goes down and stays down. It is done.
- **Banal (the corrupt killer).** He stops moving. That is all killing is, in the end. You
  wipe the blade. There is somewhere else to be.

### 5. Morning
- **Luminous.** Morning comes the way it always has and always will — indifferent to your
  deserving and generous anyway: grey going to rose going to gold, a bird trying out the
  first notes of something, the world remade once more and handed to you for free.
- **Grounded.** Morning comes, grey then pale gold. A bird starts up somewhere. A new day.
- **Banal.** It is morning. The light is back. Another day. It is the same as the others.

## A finer gradient (five bands, one beat)

To help you decide 3 bands vs. 5. The beat: **stepping out of a building into the street.**

- **Radiant (saint).** You step out and the street is *given* to you — the ordinary morning
  noise of people who do not know your name and would help you anyway, the light lying
  warm along the stones, a whole small world going about the patient business of being
  worth saving.
- **Warm (virtuous).** You step out into the street. The morning's going about its business
  — a cart, a greeting, the smell of bread from somewhere — and it is good to be in it.
- **Grounded (neutral).** You step out into the street. Carts, voices, the smell of bread.
  The settlement is awake around you.
- **Flat (corrupt).** You step into the street. Carts. Voices. Bread, somewhere. People,
  moving. You note the exits.
- **Hollow (damned).** Out. The street is a corridor between where you were and where you
  are going. Things move in it. None of them are for you, and you are not for them.

Note how the *facts* never change across the bands — the cart, the bread, the people are
always there. Only what the prose is *allowed to make of them* changes. That is the whole
mechanic, and it is the Camera Rule from the other side: the world withholds its beauty
from the one who chose power over it, and pours it out on the one who didn't.

## Decisions I need from you (the calibration is taste)
1. **Three bands or five?** Three is cleaner to author and tune; five gives a truer slow
   slide. (I lean five for the *light* side — there's a real difference between a good
   person and a saint — and three for the dark, because banality doesn't have many shades:
   it's flat, then flatter, then gone.)
2. **Where does a fresh character sit?** I've put neutral = Grounded, with beauty earned
   and banality fallen. Alternative: start slightly Warm so the world feels inviting on day
   one, and only corruption drains it. (I lean Grounded-start: beauty should be earned.)
3. **Is the Banal register too cold to *read*?** Re-read #2 and #4 banal as a player. Does
   it land as loss (right) or as boring (wrong)? Push it whichever way feels true to you —
   I can make it warmer-flat or colder-hollow.
4. **Scope:** retone the ambient/scene/action narration (yes), never the mechanics lines
   (no). Combat beats — retone them too? (I think yes; #4 above is the strongest argument
   for the whole feature.)
5. **The Creator's note:** the *most* beautiful prose should be reserved for the desireless
   acts — the kindness with no witness, the friend's hard truth. Should "Radiant" be
   reachable only on the light path proper, or also in those singular desireless moments
   regardless of band? (I lean: those moments briefly unlock Radiant for anyone, because
   that is exactly when the silent god is near.)
