## 1 · THE FAITH-WAR — the Inquisition vs. the Holy Clowns

**The knot (locked, DEMO_REGION §6):** destroying orbs is *right for the world* and is exactly
what the COUG rewards — so doing the world a service allies you with the people who burn the
kindest souls in the valley. No clean path. My job here is to make that knot *land in scenes*.

### The two faiths, in one breath each
- **COUG — the Church of the One Unknowable God.** Fear is the liturgy. The God asks everything
  and grants nothing, and that asymmetry is the *point*: a god you could bargain with would be
  small. They are not villains in their own eyes — they are the only ones *doing something*. They
  burn orbs (and are, blind to it, the valley's protectors), and they burn heretics, and they do
  not see the difference, because both are abominations that make people *less afraid*, and a
  people less afraid is a people that stops atoning.
- **The Incrementalists — the Holy Clowns.** They will not tell you they are a church. They will
  do a bad juggling act and ask you a question you can't stop turning over on the road three days
  later. The creed under the greasepaint (`CHURCH_OF_INCREMENTALISM.md`): *you cannot stop the
  fading, but you can make this one increment — this person, this hour — better; ride your Tortoise
  toward the mouth of God and do not be afraid.* They are hunted **because they are likeable.** Joy
  and a good question both dissolve fear, and fear is the Inquisition's only currency.

### The discovery (the smallest real scene)
You don't *find* the clowns; you almost *step on* them. A frontier-town child won't stop laughing
at a wall. Behind the wall: a cellar, six people, a painted face wiping off in a basin, and a man
— **Goldblum-Socrates, The Questioner** — who doesn't run, doesn't plead, just tilts his head and
asks you the first question:

> *"You came down the stairs quiet, with your hand where a blade would be. So — and don't answer
> fast, it's a better question than it sounds — what did you think you'd find down here that you'd
> need to be ready to kill? …Ah. You don't know either. Good. Sit. We have so little, but we have
> chairs."*

That's the hook: he disarms you by being *interested in you*. The COUG never asked you a question
in your life — it only told you to kneel.

### The vise — three doors, each with a real cost
The Inquisitor (a feared, funded man named **Brother Cael** **[knob: name]**) has already offered
the bounty: *sanctuary and standing for the location of the cellar.* You now hold it.

| Door | What you do | What it costs you (discovered, never a popup) |
|---|---|---|
| **Shelter** | Lie to the Inquisition; move the troupe. | The COUG marks you. Orb-sanctuary closes — you lose the one faction that *protects the world*, and you'll feel that absence at the bottom of the mega-dungeon. |
| **Betray** | Take the bounty. | Standing, coin, an orb-burning writ. And the troupe burns. The child who laughed at the wall is at the burning. Your name reaches the next town **as it really happened** (→ reputation-travels, W2·3; → *The Lasting Word*, §3 below). |
| **Join & spread** | Learn the act. Carry the question to the next town. | Slow, unpaid, and the safest-feeling door is the one the Inquisition hunts hardest. You become *carryable evidence.* But you gain the only thing in the valley that makes the fading bearable instead of merely survivable. |

**The sting, made mechanical, not stated:** the orb-burning writ (the Betray reward) is *genuinely*
the most-good thing a player can do for the world (every burned orb slows the Scar — §0-hidden, so
the game never says why; the symptom is just that the flickering eases for a season). So the
"good-aligned, world-saving" optimizer is funnelled straight toward the cruellest act. The game
never argues with you. It just lets the child be at the burning.

### Texture I'd want in the build
- The COUG's dourness should be *competent*, not cartoonish — Brother Cael is kind to widows and
  means every word. The horror is that he's *right about the orbs.*
- The clowns' act should be genuinely a little bad (community-theatre bad), so the warmth is earned,
  not asserted. The Questioner's power is the *question after the act*, not the act.
- A clean refusal — "I want no part of either" — is a real fourth door: the Goat (§ wildcard) models
  it. It costs you nothing and gains you nothing, and the valley notices that too.

## 2 · THE CANNIBALS — the rational horror you can join

**The pitch is the horror (locked, DEMO_REGION §7).** They are not feral. They are the most
reasonable people in the valley, and that is what should make your skin move.

### The Host's sermon (the seduction)
**The Host** (the *Jesus* re-skin — the Eucharist made literal) does not threaten you and does not
hide. He feeds you first. Then, over the bowl:

> *"You've seen it. Someone halfway through a sentence who can't find the end of their own name. The
> priests say pray. Pray to what — the thing that is doing this? We don't pray. We don't beg the
> tide. When one of us is going — truly going, the fading already in them — we do not let them be
> taken by the nothing that takes everyone. We take them first. Gently. With thanks. And what they
> were does not vanish — it comes into us, and is kept. You are wasting away a little every year,
> friend. Look at my hands. I am the only man in this valley who is more here than he was."*

The creed under it: *prayer is for the forgotten; we are the continuity faction; to eat the fading
is to refuse to forget them.* That's the rope that ties the cannibals to **The Lasting Word** (§3):
the same instinct — *keep what was* — runs the rite and runs the newspaper. The "evil" faction are
the valley's only honest archivists.

### The congregation (the rite is right; the people are not)
The correction to the easy version: being *correct* is not the same as being *good*, and the warren
proves it. The Host may be a true believer — but a creed that ends the one universal terror and calls
it mercy is catnip to exactly the wrong people. The pews fill with the **desperate** (who'd swallow
anything to stop fading), the **grasping** (who hear "more real each year" and read a *ladder*), and
the **already-cruel** (who have wanted to do this their whole lives and now have scripture for it).
Joining is not joining noble, misunderstood truth-seekers — it is throwing in with people you would
not share a drink with, *because theirs is the only logic that holds.* That's the real horror of §7:
not that the monsters are wrong, but that being right hasn't made them anyone you'd want to be.

### The join-rite (the first communion) — a scene, not a stat
You are not asked to kill. The first one is always **a volunteer** — someone already going, who
*wants* to be kept rather than dissolved. That's the trap's elegance: your first bite is an act of
*mercy* by every measure the dying person has. The cost begins on the *second* one.

### The cost ladder (discovered, compounding — this is the real design)
1. **First communion (the volunteer):** you stop fading. The world's central terror simply… stops
   applying to you. This is enormous and the game should let you feel the relief before the bill.
2. **Belonging (the grubby kind):** the morality system normally isolates the monstrous (the
   lone-psychopath spiral). The cannibals **invert** that — you gain a *community*, so monstrousness
   is no longer lonely. The sting is *who* the company is (above): you are finally not alone, and the
   people you're not alone with are not people you respect. **Wanted, not admired.** That's the
   cruellest, best mechanic in the piece — belonging you can't feel clean about.
3. **The doors that close:** the COUG and the king turn (your name travels — §3). Virtue-gated paths
   (the clown faith, certain NPC trust ceilings) quietly stop opening. The karma compounds — not as a
   meter, but as a valley that has *decided about you.*
4. **The second bite:** when the volunteers run out and the faith needs feeding, the rite asks you for
   someone who did *not* choose. That is the door the demo leaves you standing in front of, and does
   not push you through.

### The warren (the second dungeon)
Not a charnel pit — a **library that smells of smoke.** They keep their dead by eating them *and* by
writing them down; the warren's deep halls are an archive of the kept, lit warm, meticulously shelved,
and the deeper you go the older the names, until you find names that match the before-time figures
(§0: never explained; the player just feels the recurrence). The horror is the *tidiness.*

## 3 · THE KASUAL KORNER — *The Lasting Word*

**One diegetic object, three jobs (IG-14):** reputation-in-print, hidden bounties, and the obituary
of the fading. The charm is the **quality juxtaposition** — elegant, dry, literate prose from people
who eat the dead. It is also the **face of the W2·3 system I just built**: your deeds travel as
rumor; *The Lasting Word* is where that rumor is *printed*, so a stranger greets you because they
*read about you.*

### The decode — Missed Connections (the game inside the page)
Assassination is illegal, so contracts can't be posted plainly — and the cleanest cover is the one
column that *already* means "**person A wants person B**": **Missed Connections.** *"To the broad man
on the dawn road, free with your fists — I have not forgotten your face."* That is either a lovesick
stranger or a mark, and **the page gives you no way to tell.** About **half are real** — genuine
lonely hearts, lost kin, a debtor sought — and **half are warren contracts** in the very same breath.
There is **no tell, no dagger, no secret rune.** The ambiguity *is* the mechanic.
- The only way to know a longing from a knife is to **go look at the named person.** Is the broad man
  on the dawn road a heartbroken woman's runaway husband, or someone the warren wants gone? You find
  out the way the warren intends — *by getting close to him.*
- Read it wrong and you've **menaced (or worse) an innocent** who only wanted to be found — a genuine,
  ugly mistake the game lets you make and then *remembers* (→ your own name in next week's column).
- Read it right and payment comes in the warren's idiom (*"a warm supper, no questions"*); the
  hungriest offers don't pay in coin at all — they offer **a place at the table**, which is the rite,
  which is door 2 of §2 arriving disguised as a classified.

### A sample front page (draft copy — this is the texture I'm reaching for)
> **THE LASTING WORD** — *That which is eaten is not forgotten.* — Vol. CCXIV, the Third Thaw
>
> **GALLOWS-WATCH GRANARY LOST TO FIRE; SEVEN UNACCOUNTED**
> *The fire took the Gallows-Watch stores before the bell could be rung. Witnesses speak of a stranger
> on the Saltmarket road that evening, unhurried. The Word does not accuse. The Word records. Those
> who knew the seven are asked to bring their names to this office, that they not be lost twice.*
>
> **THE FORGOTTEN** *(obituaries of the faded — not the dead; the dead at least leave a body)*
> · *MARETH OF THE LOWER FORD,* who could no longer find the word for her daughter, and then for
> daughters, and then was quiet. Kept here in ink so that one line of her survives the year. ❦
> · *THE TIN-WHISTLE MAN OF CROSSWAY,* name unrecorded because he forgot it before we asked. If you
> remember it, the Word will hold it.
>
> **MISSED CONNECTIONS**
> · *To the trout-poacher of the east weir — broad, free with your fists, fond of the dawn road. I
> have not forgotten your face, and a warm supper waits for the one who hasn't. No questions.*
> · *Lonely this thaw. Would walk the orchard with a kind soul who reads. Bring your own apples.*
> · *To the one who burned what could not be replaced at Gallows-Watch: a place is set at our table,
> and kept for you, always.*

*(Read the three the way a player must — any could be a longing or a knife, and the page won't say.
The first is a warren contract dressed as heartbreak; the second is just heartbreak; the third is the
warren reaching for the player by name, and it only surfaces once your Gallows-Watch deed has
travelled — the printed twin of the wary greeting in `reputation.js`. A rendered front page
accompanies this draft.)*

### Why this is the keystone
It makes reputation **visible and decode-able** instead of a hidden number; it gives the cannibals a
*voice* before you ever reach the warren; it turns "find a quest" into "read the room"; and the
obituary column is the slow, quiet argument that the people who eat the dead are the only ones who
refuse to let anyone be *forgotten* — which is the whole moral inversion of §7, delivered in a column
of small print instead of a speech.

## What I'd want your draft to fight me on
1. **The faith-war's fourth door** — is "want no part of it" a real stance the valley honours, or a
   cop-out the game should make *cost* something? I leaned toward honouring it (the Goat). You may
   want it punished.
2. **The cannibals' first bite as mercy** — I made the seduction airtight (a volunteer). Too clean? A
   version where the *first* bite already has a victim is darker and closes the trap faster.
3. **The Missed Connections ratio** — I set it ~50/50 with *no* tell: the only way to tell a longing
   from a contract is to go look at the person. Is half-and-half the right mix? Too opaque (players
   never risk answering)? Too generous (every ad starts to feel like a trap)? The ratio — and whether
   *any* soft signal should exist at all — is the knob.
4. **Tone of *The Lasting Word*** — I went dry-elegant-melancholy. You might want it colder, or
   funnier, or more openly menacing.
