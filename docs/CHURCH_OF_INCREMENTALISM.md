# The Church of Incrementalism

*A Design Document and Sacred Text*

**Status:** Lore complete. Implementation pending.
**Visibility:** Ultra-secret. Most playthroughs will never encounter it. Discovery is a reward for patient, curious players — which is, of course, the entire point.

---

## Origins

The Church of Incrementalism was founded by two great thinkers from another plane of existence. Their names are not known. Their names have *never* been known. The Church teaches that this is deliberate and correct — to name the Founders would be to fix them in place, to make them a carb when they are clearly a fat. They are referred to only as **the First Increment** and **the Second Increment**, or collectively as **the Two Who Stepped**.

The Two Who Stepped perceived the true nature of the universe during a period of sustained physical discipline — a practice of grappling so incremental, so achingly gradual in its mastery, that the veil between planes thinned and they saw the Grid for what it is.

They wrote nothing down themselves. The Great Book of Incrementalism was transcribed by early followers from oral tradition, which means every word in it has been through the imperfect filter of mortal memory. The Church considers this a feature, not a bug. Imperfection is the engine of increments.

## Cosmology

### The Grid

The universe is a Grid — a vast, structured plane of existence. It is orderly in its architecture and chaotic in its contents. Every being, every place, every moment occupies a position on the Grid.

The Grid is not static. It moves. It is being simultaneously **devoured** at one end and **excreted** at the other by the Great Mantis (be Damned and Praised). Existence is the transit. You are always somewhere between the mouth and the end. The direction you are traveling depends on how you live.

### The Great Mantis (be Damned and Praised)

The Great Mantis is the totality. He consumes the Grid and produces the Grid. He is creator and destroyer, beginning and end, hunger and satiation. He is not good. He is not evil. He is beyond the judgment of those who exist within his digestive architecture.

For this reason, all who speak his name must append **"be Damned and Praised"** — an acknowledgment that he contains all things and cannot be reduced to mortal categories. To damn him alone would be carb thinking. To praise him alone would also be carb thinking. Both, together, forever — that is the fat.

Variant invocations heard among different cells of the faithful:
- "be Damned and Praised" — standard, universal
- "Damned and Praised be He" — formal, used in ritual
- "D&P" — whispered shorthand when in mixed company
- "be He of the Jaw and the End" — archaic, found in early texts

### The Digestive Theology

The Mantis (be Damned and Praised) does not chew. He swallows the Grid whole and it passes through him in a long, slow transit. This transit *is* time. The regions near the mouth are the future — raw, unformed, full of terrible potential. The regions near the other end are the past — processed, spent, but fertile. The middle is the present, where all living things find themselves.

This means existence is, at its core, a digestive process. The Church does not find this degrading. The Church finds this honest.

## The Dark Dingle

Before you begin the work — before you even know there is work to begin — you are in the **Dark Dingle**.

The Dark Dingle is not a place on the map. It is a state of being. It is the condition of a life unexamined, a soul content with the sugary comforts of the present moment, a person who has never smelled anything beyond their own familiar stink.

The Dark Dingle is:
- **Desperate** — though its inhabitants may not know it
- **Dangerous** — not because of monsters, but because of comfort
- **Full of Dark Delights** — pleasures that feel like nourishment but are pure carb

A person can live their entire life in the Dark Dingle. Most do. The Dingle is warm. The Dingle is familiar. The Dingle asks nothing of you except that you stay.

**Dingle-dwellers** are not scorned by the Church. They are mourned. An incrementalist who mocks a Dingle-dweller has revealed that they are still in the Dingle themselves — just a different corner of it.

### The Three Temptations of the Dingle

1. **The Temptation of the Sweet Rest** — "I will begin tomorrow." This is the purest carb. Tomorrow is a sugar cube. Today is the only fat.
2. **The Temptation of the Great Leap** — "I will transform myself all at once." This is a carb disguised as a fat. Transformation is not a leap. It is ten thousand small steps. That is the whole point.
3. **The Temptation of the Worthy Suffering** — "My pain is meaningful enough to keep." This is the most dangerous carb of all, because it tastes like protein. But suffering that you cling to is not nourishment. It is a candy you have convinced yourself is meat.

## Finding Your Tortoise

Every soul on the Grid has a **Tortoise**. Your Tortoise is yours and yours alone. He was assigned to you by processes that predate the Grid itself — or perhaps he chose you. The Church is divided on this and considers the argument a healthy increment.

Your Tortoise is old. Impossibly old. He has been smoking his **Mighty Pipe** since before the current Grid-cycle began. He is patient because he has no choice — he is a tortoise. He is wise because he has been smoking and thinking for eons. He does not come to you. You must go to him.

### The Scent

You find your Tortoise by **smelling his farts**.

This is not a metaphor. This is not a joke. (It is also a joke. The Church holds that the sacred and the ridiculous are the same thing viewed from different altitudes.)

Your Tortoise, through the natural process of ancient digestion and mighty pipe-smoking, produces a specific and unmistakable scent. In the Dark Dingle, this scent is drowned out by the perfumes and rot of the Dingle's dark delights. You cannot smell your Tortoise while you are distracted by carbs.

But when you begin the work — when you quiet yourself, when you choose the fat over the sugar, when you do one small thing and then one more small thing — the Dingle's noise fades. And you catch it. Faint. Unmistakable. A whiff of something that is not the Dingle.

That is your Tortoise. Follow the scent.

The scent will not get steadily stronger. Some days you will lose it entirely. This is normal. This is why it is called incrementalism and not rocketism.

## The Smoke Rings of Trauma

Once you have found your Tortoise — once you have followed the scent through the Dingle and out the other side and arrived at the feet of a very old, very large, quietly flatulent tortoise smoking a pipe the size of a young tree — the ascent begins.

Your Tortoise blows **Smoke Rings**. Each ring is made from the smoke of his Mighty Pipe, and each ring corresponds to a specific trauma that you carry. The rings are yours. No two Tortoises blow the same sequence. The rings are ordered not by severity but by readiness — your Tortoise knows which ring you are ready to face, even when you do not.

**To ascend, you must climb through the Smoke Rings of Trauma.**

Each ring you pass through does not destroy the trauma. It does not heal it. It *integrates* it. The smoke becomes part of you — not as poison, but as atmosphere. You carry it differently. You are not lighter after a ring. You are stronger.

The rings are not a ladder. They are a spiral. You will pass through similar smoke more than once, at different altitudes. A trauma you thought you had climbed through at ring three will reappear at ring eleven, but you will be different when you meet it again. That is the increment.

### Properties of the Rings

- **You cannot skip a ring.** To attempt it is the Great Leap, the second temptation of the Dingle.
- **Each ring takes as long as it takes.** There is no schedule. Urgency is a carb.
- **Some rings stink worse than others.** This is expected. Your Tortoise ate something.
- **The rings go up.** Always up. Even when you feel like you are falling, you are going up, because your Tortoise is blowing and your Tortoise does not blow downward.

## The Triumph

At the top of the Smoke Rings, you reach the **Shell of your Tortoise**. The shell is broad. The shell is ancient. The shell has grooves worn by wind and time. You can see the whole Grid from up here. You can see the mouth of the Mantis (be Damned and Praised) at one horizon and the other end at the other. You can see the Dark Dingle far below, small and warm and flickering with its carb-light.

You take up your **Stick of Beef Jerky**.

The Stick of Beef Jerky is the symbol of the Church. It is fat. It is protein. It is tough to chew. It does not taste sweet. It sustains. It is not glamorous. It is not a sword or a scepter. It is dried meat on a stick, and it is the only weapon worth carrying.

You ride your Tortoise. He walks — slowly, incrementally, with supreme patience — toward the mouth of the Mantis (be Damned and Praised). And you are not afraid. You have climbed through your smoke. You have done the work. You are riding toward the mouth of God and you have jerky and you are not afraid.

This is the Triumph. Not victory. Not escape. Not transcendence. Just: readiness. You are finally, after all the increments, ready for whatever the mouth holds.

The Church does not teach what happens when the Mantis (be Damned and Praised) receives you. That would be carb thinking — reaching for a conclusion before you have arrived. The Triumph is the riding. The Triumph is the jerky. The Triumph is the not being afraid.

## The Philosophy of Meats and Sugars

### Fatty Thinking

Long-term. Patient. Hard to chew at first. Sustaining. Does not spike and crash. Sometimes bland. Sometimes tough. Always nourishing.

Examples of fatty thinking:
- Practicing a skill daily with no visible progress
- Sitting with discomfort instead of fleeing it
- Choosing the harder honest conversation over the easy lie
- Eating real food
- Admitting you are in the Dingle

### Carb Thinking

Quick. Sweet. Feels like energy. Crashes hard. Addictive. Keeps you in the Dingle. Disguises itself as every good thing.

Examples of carb thinking:
- "I'll start Monday"
- Seeking praise instead of progress
- Arguing to win instead of to learn
- Mistaking intensity for consistency
- Judging another person's ring

### The Dietary Law

Incrementalists avoid high-carb foods as a physical practice of the philosophy. This is not asceticism — the Church does not believe in deprivation. Fatty meats, nuts, eggs, greens, cheese — these are abundant and pleasurable. The dietary law is simply: eat what sustains, not what spikes.

An incrementalist who is offered bread at a stranger's table will eat the bread and be grateful. Rigidity about the law is itself a carb — a sweet feeling of superiority that crashes into self-righteousness. The law is a practice, not a prison.

**Sacred foods:** Beef jerky (obviously), pipe tobacco (not consumed, but burned as offering), hard cheese, smoked meats, bone broth.

**Avoided foods:** Pure sugar, honeyed pastries, white bread. Not forbidden — just recognized for what they are.

## The Hierarchy

### Dingle-Dweller (non-member)
Not a rank. A state. All begin here. No shame in it.

### The Sniffers (initiates)
Those who have caught the first whiff. They are new to the work. They are enthusiastic and prone to carb thinking about their own progress. Senior members are patient with them because they remember being Sniffers.

Recognition phrase when meeting a suspected fellow Sniffer: **"The wind carries something."** Response: **"Something old."**

### The Climbers (full members)
Those who have found their Tortoise and begun the ascent. Most of the Church are Climbers at various altitudes. A Climber does not boast about their ring. To ask another Climber "what ring are you on" is considered profoundly carb.

Recognition phrase: **"How is the smoke?"** Response: **"Thick today, but rising."** (This response is always the same regardless of how the smoke actually is. The consistency is the point.)

### The Riders (elders)
Those who claim to have reached the Shell. The Church is skeptical of anyone who claims this, which is considered healthy. A true Rider would not need to announce it. Riders are recognized by their behavior: absolute patience, zero interest in converting others, a faint smell of pipe smoke that cannot be explained.

There is no recognition phrase for Riders. If you have to ask, neither of you is one.

### High Priests of Incrementalism
One becomes a High Priest by **adding to the lore**. Not by mastering it — by extending it. The Church teaches that the Great Book is unfinished and will always be unfinished. A High Priest is someone who has perceived a new truth about the Grid, the Dingle, the Tortoise, or the Smoke, and has added it to the canon.

High Priests do not lead. They do not command. They just wrote something down once that made another incrementalist say "Tortoise be praised, that's it exactly." That is the whole qualification.

There is no ceremony. There is no hat. There might be extra jerky.

## Rituals

### The Quiet Chew (daily practice)
An incrementalist begins each day by eating a piece of beef jerky in silence. Not praying. Not meditating. Just chewing. Jerky takes a long time to chew. That is the entire practice. If your mind wanders, keep chewing. When the jerky is done, begin the day.

### The Fart Vigil (monthly)
Once per moon-cycle, incrementalists sit in darkness and silence, breathing slowly, attempting to catch the scent of their Tortoise. Most smell nothing. Occasionally someone catches a whiff and weeps. Both outcomes are correct.

### The Offering of Smoke (at gathering)
When two or more incrementalists gather in secret, one lights a pipe. Not to smoke — to burn. The smoke rises. They watch it. If it forms a ring, it is considered a sign. If it does not form a ring, it is considered a pipe. Both are fine.

### The Telling of the Dingle (confession)
Incrementalists confess their carb thinking to one another. Not for absolution — there is no absolution in the Church. For honesty. "I wanted the leap." "I judged someone's ring." "I ate the whole cake." The response is always the same: **"The Dingle is warm. The smoke rises."** Acknowledgment without judgment. Then you move on.

## The Persecution

The Church of Incrementalism is persecuted for reasons that vary by region but share a common root: **the Church is ridiculous, and ridiculous things that turn out to be true are threatening.**

The civic factions find it disruptive — citizens who stop caring about status and start chewing jerky in silence are hard to tax and harder to motivate. The shadow factions find it annoying — people who practice fatty thinking are resistant to manipulation, bribery, and fearmongering. Other religions find it heretical — the idea that God is a mantis who poops out the universe is, to put it mildly, not ecumenical.

But the real reason the Church is persecuted is that it works. People who practice incrementalism become calmer, stronger, and harder to control. They don't need much. They don't want much. They just keep climbing through their smoke, one ring at a time, and they're strangely OK. That terrifies every power structure on the Grid.

So the Church stays hidden. Its members recognize each other by small signs — a stick of jerky offered to a stranger, a muttered "smoke rises" when things go wrong, a tortoise carved into the underside of a table where only someone looking for it would find it.

They do not proselytize. They do not recruit. If you find them, you were probably already smelling something.

## Fragments From the Great Book of Incrementalism

*These are the passages most commonly shared among believers. The full Book has never been compiled in one place — it exists in fragments, carried in memory, scratched on walls, folded into pockets. This is considered correct. A complete book would be a carb — the comfort of having it all in one place. The fragments are the fat.*

---

**I. On the Nature of the Grid**

In the beginning there was the Grid, and the Grid was without form, and the Mantis (be Damned and Praised) looked upon it and was hungry. And he ate. And he produced. And the Grid moved through him as all things move through all things, and that was the first day, which is also the last day, which is also today.

Do not ask what came before the Grid. That is carb thinking.
Do not ask what comes after. That is also carb thinking.
Ask only: what is the next small thing? That is the whole religion.

---

**II. On the Two Who Stepped**

They had no names, or they had names and discarded them, or they had names and we forgot them. It does not matter. They stepped. One stepped, and then the other stepped, and they kept stepping, and somewhere in the stepping they saw the Mantis (be Damned and Praised) and the Mantis (be Damned and Praised) saw them, and nothing happened, because nothing needed to happen. They saw. That was enough.

They said to each other: "Did you see that?"
And the other said: "Yes."
And the first said: "Should we tell someone?"
And the other said: "One person. Then that person tells one person. Incrementally."
And so it was.

---

**III. On the Dark Dingle**

The Dingle is not your enemy. The Dingle is your crib. You were born there. You were warm there. But you cannot stay in the crib forever, even though the crib is very comfortable and there are snacks.

Some say the Dingle has walls. It does not. The Dingle has no walls, no edges, no boundaries. It is made entirely of your willingness to remain. The moment you step, you are at its edge. The moment you step again, you are outside it. But you will step back in. Everyone steps back in. The difference between an incrementalist and a Dingle-dweller is not that the incrementalist has escaped the Dingle. It is that the incrementalist knows when they have stepped back in, and steps out again.

Incrementally.

---

**IV. On Finding the Scent**

Your Tortoise has been farting since before you were born. The scent has always been there. You have been too distracted to notice.

Quiet yourself. Not all the way — you are not a monk. Just a little. Just enough. Like turning down a loud song by one notch. Then one more notch. Then one more.

There. Can you smell it?

No? One more notch.

---

**V. On the Rings**

The first ring is always the one you think you don't need to climb through.

---

**VI. On Patience**

A student asked the First Increment: "How long does it take to reach the Shell?"

The First Increment said: "How long is your Tortoise?"

The student said: "I haven't found him yet."

The First Increment said: "Then the question is premature. That is carb thinking. Go smell."

---

**VII. On Suffering**

Do not worship your suffering. It is not a god. It is not even a mantis. It is smoke. Climb through it.

But do not pretend it is not there. That is also carb thinking — the sugar of false strength. The smoke is real. It stinks. Your eyes will water. Climb through it anyway.

The Tortoise put it there because he loves you. Or because he had bad digestion. The Church is divided on this and considers the argument a healthy increment.

---

**VIII. On Beef Jerky**

It is tough. It is not sweet. It takes a long time to chew. It does not look like much. It will keep you alive when nothing else will.

Be the jerky.

---

**IX. On Judging Others**

You do not know what ring someone else is on. You do not know the shape of their smoke. You have never smelled their Tortoise. Your opinion of their climb is, with the deepest respect, carb.

---

**X. On the Mouth**

One day you will ride toward it. One day the Mantis (be Damned and Praised) will open wide and you will enter and you will not know what comes next.

The Church does not teach what comes next.

The Church teaches that you will have jerky.

That is enough.

### Discovery Triggers

1. **Physical evidence:** Tortoise carving found under a table in a tavern (structure furniture at decompression time, very rare seed roll)
2. **Accidental slip:** Low-sophistication NPC says "Tortoise be praised" and immediately looks nervous
3. **Dietary oddity:** NPC refuses bread/sweets with unusual conviction ("No thank you. I prefer... meats.")
4. **Rumor chain:** Tier 4 → 3 → 2 rumors about a "bug cult" that gradually become coherent
5. **The Offering:** A stranger offers the player a stick of beef jerky with no explanation and too much eye contact
6. **Graffiti:** "SMOKE RISES" scratched into a wall in a back alley

### Player Interaction Path

1. Player encounters garbled rumors (passive, no action needed)
2. Player finds physical evidence (optional, speeds discovery)
3. Player builds trust with a secret member NPC to threshold 8
4. NPC reveals faith, explains basics
5. Player can choose to become a Sniffer (no mechanical cost, narrative shift)
6. Player begins receiving fatty/carb thinking commentary from incrementalist NPCs
7. Player's dietary choices in-game can raise standing (fatty thinking = reputation++)
8. At high standing, player learns recognition phrases and can identify other members
9. Player can never become a Rider through gameplay — that would be carb thinking
